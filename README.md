Project Title: vector Spherical-harmonic beam Optimization for Forward modeled Antennas

In this research project, the goal is to determine the antenna beam which would be most optimal/sensitive to making a measurement of the global 21 centimeter signal. There are multiple features which we believe to impact antenna sensitivity. Chromaticity is implicit in antenna design and is likely needed to be limited to levels which vary sky measurements below that of the believed 21 centimeter cosmological signal. In addition, the ionosphere will reflect and refract incoming radiowaves, especially at the band of the believed dark ages dip. This project specifically looks to optimize for the dark ages dip but will be encompasing of the full band (1-150mhz) for most of the testing. 

The process of the code for this research will go as follows:

Outer loop:
1. Establish electromagnetic radiation pattern in basis of vector spherical harmonics which has had image theory imposed at a certain distance above a ground plane. 
2. Fit for linear coefficients using a loss function of (theta hat - theta) where theta are the cosmological parameters from the 21cm signal. We will inject the signal in the form of a gaussian dip with varing amplitude, width, and mean. This ensures that the fitting doesnt just generate a beam which artificially induces a signal. (aka. if an amplitude of zero is given as the signal amplitude there should be zero recovery)

Inner loop: (This is where the theta parameters are extracted)
1. Initialize a sky map which is void of 21 cm signal.
2. Create multiple initializations with varying signal parameters are 24 LST's
3. Use antenna temperature equation to integrate beam with sky then normalize
4. Fit (in a bayesian sense) a foreground model + 21cm signal + noise parameters


HOW TO MAKE MAPS:
We will make sky maps using a reference of two GSM2016 maps (at 408mhz and 230mhz) (subtracting and adding the Tcmb when necessary). From this we can create a map of the spectral index per healpx pixel. Then we will split the pixels into 10 quartile bins and assign the pixels the mean spectral index of the quartile region. Then utilizing a model comprised of spectral index, amplitude, and spectral curvature (as used in https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.00299v1), we can generate maps at any frequency using those mean spectral indexs of the quartile region. 

HOW TO GET ANTENNA TEMPERATURE:
We will take initializations of 24 LST bins starting at midnight 10/21/2026 to midnight 10/22/2026. Then we will apply a sky map characterized by the maximum electron density within a column of the atmosphere which will transform the sky by the requisit refraction caused by the ionosphere at each frequency.
